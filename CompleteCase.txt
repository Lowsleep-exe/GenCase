=== DEDUCTION CHAIN ===

Evidence 1 establishes X (Dr. Voss entering the library at night with gloves and a briefcase).

Evidence 2 establishes Y (Ms. Lin's presence in the break room during the theft).

X + Y establishes Z (Dr. Voss and Ms. Lin are involved in the theft).

Evidence 3 contradicts Dr. Voss (Dr. Voss's alibi and his claim of work in the library are not verified).

Evidence 4 establishes that Mr. Krasnov could not have committed the crime (Krasnov was caught carrying the manuscript on the day of the theft).

Evidence 5 establishes that Ms. Lin's alibi is not credible (she was caught with the manuscript).

Therefore, Dr. Voss and Ms. Lin are the only suspects left.

The final deduction identifies Dr. Voss as the perpetrator.

=== COMPLETE SOLUTION ===

Dr. Marcus Voss, a colleague of Prof. Blackwood, has been secretly trading rare manuscripts on the black market. He used his frequent night visits to the library to gain access and enter the rare books room. Dr. Voss saw the manuscript and decided to take it, planning to sell it later. On the day of the theft, he used his gloves and briefcase to avoid detection. He then used Ms. Lin’s presence in the break room as a red herring. Ms. Lin was carrying the manuscript as part of her project, but it was actually the one he stole. He lied about his activities and claimed he was just working in the library.
